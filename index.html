A LIFE IN MANY CHAPTERS

One name.
Many chapters.

Imran Khan's public life has crossed the worlds of international cricket, charitable work, education and national politics.

Born in Lahore in 1952, he rose to international prominence as a cricketer and captained Pakistan to its first men's Cricket World Cup title in 1992. He later became known for his work supporting cancer care and education, before founding a political party and becoming prime minister in 2018.

His legacy is influential, complex and contested. This archive presents key milestones as a starting point for exploring the achievements, decisions and debates surrounding his life.

03 / THE DEFINING MOMENT

The year Pakistan
made history.

The 1992 Cricket World Cup in Australia and New Zealand remains one of the landmark moments in Pakistan's sporting history. Imran Khan captained the side through a difficult group stage and into a memorable tournament victory.

Pakistan defeated England in the final at the Melbourne Cricket Ground on 25 March 1992. The victory helped cement Khan's place in the country's cricketing story.

06 / THE POLITICAL CHAPTER

From the pitch
to public office.

After retiring from international cricket, Khan entered politics and established Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI) in 1996. The party spent years building its organisation and electoral support.

PTI emerged as a major electoral force and won the largest number of seats in the 2018 National Assembly election. Khan became prime minister that year. His government faced economic pressures, policy disputes and political opposition. He left office in April 2022 following a parliamentary vote of no confidence.
